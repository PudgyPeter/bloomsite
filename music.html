Album 2025

The Light We Chase

Second album. Eleven songs about memory, place and the cost of chasing a light, written and recorded in a handful of weeks in Sydney.

Released
31 October 2025 Label
Pure Noise Records Producer
Sam Bassal Tracks
11
Listen on Spotify Tracklist & lyrics↓
Tracklist 11 songs · 34 min 34 sec

Bloom's second album arrived on 31 October 2025 through Pure Noise Records, written largely in a burst of January sessions and finished with producer Sam Bassal in April. Where Maybe In Another Life followed a character through fantasy and self-destruction, The Light We Chase turns outward: to the suburb the band grew up in, to the friends they swore they would never forget, and to the cost of eight years spent chasing a dream.

It is also the band's widest record. Shoegaze haze, stadium-sized choruses, acoustic tenderness and some of their heaviest material sit side by side, with guest turns from Patrick Miranda (Movements), Jack Bergin (Void Of Vision) and Mikaila Delgado (Yours Truly).

The album is built from homages. Belrose answers the opening line of An Entry; Act II is a sequel to Sink Into The Soil; Glen Street nods back to the first EPs. Even the title is a kind of thesis: the light is worth chasing, even when it burns.

About the song

A scene-setting introduction named after the suburb where the band grew up. Jono has said he wanted the album to open the way The 1975 records do, with a track that speaks to the whole record rather than standing apart from it. The invitation, “walk back with me”, deliberately answers “step into my state of mind” from An Entry.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. Walk back with me / And I’ll show you everything

    The album's first line is a hand extended to the listener, and a direct callback to the opening of Maybe In Another Life. Jono called it “a really beautiful way to be like: hey, I’m going to let you in on a place that I hold a lot of importance in”.

  2. The mulberry tree / So far away

    The mulberry tree was a real tree on the walk home from school. The cemetery and the powerlines are real landmarks too, name-dropped early so the record is anchored in an actual place rather than a vague idea of home.

About the song

A song about the fear of forgetting — faces, names, nights — and the knowledge that memory erodes whether we like it or not. Jack has said the band wanted something experimental early in the tracklist so listeners would know this record would be wider than a normal metalcore release; the ending was built by Sam Bassal dragging elements around in the piano roll at random, then refining the chaos.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. I’m afraid of the years starting to blur / Afraid of forgetting who we were

    The song's central anxiety: not death, but the slow softening of detail. The chorus turns the fear into a promise — “to all of my old friends, the ones I swore I won’t forget”.

  2. Studio note

    The band tested every BPM between 180 and 185 by physically jumping in the room, looking for the speed a crowd could bounce to as one. It is, in Jono's words, “focus grouped”.

About the song

Named for Glen Street in Belrose, and for the theatre where the band performed school productions before they were a band. Jono calls it the record's most traditional Bloom song: a big opening, a drop, then a swelling melodic-hardcore finish in the lineage of Past Tense and In Passing. Jack wrote the chorus after watching 10 Things I Hate About You.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. I still walk by Glen Street / And see you standing next to me

    The song maps a relationship onto a suburban street: first kiss, last kiss, and every ordinary walk in between. The narrator cannot make the place forget what happened there.

  2. Your mind’s a liar, a cheat / It keeps a short memory for the bitter / And a long for the sweet

    A line the narrator repeats to himself until it becomes a kind of curse. He wants to hate the person, and can't — the mind keeps the sweetness and lets the bitterness fade.

About the song

The album's most referential song: a sequel to Sink Into The Soil, built around that song's lead riff, with the earlier line “desperate to be like them” turned into “desperate to be like you”. Its working title was literally SITS II. It opens side two, sits inside the record's theatre imagery, and features Jack Bergin of Void Of Vision.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. I am waiting in the wings / For someone to let me in / I’m the understudy

    The theatre metaphor turns envy into an identity: always watching, never chosen. The title works three ways — second side, second act of the album's narrative, and second version of Sink Into The Soil.

  2. A knife in my hand / For the headline / The perfect night / To take the limelight

    The understudy's fantasy becomes a violent curtain call. “My final act” ends the song with the sense that the performance and the destruction are the same gesture.

About the song

The album's hardest song to write, and its most direct: eight years of being a band, the strain on friendships, finances and lives outside the group. Jono has said it is not a complaint about fans but an honest look at what touring and the industry cost. The closing image — “Withered me, never bloomed” — turns the band's name into a threat.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. Eight years of my life / Dedicated / Eight years of my life / Desolated

    Bloom formed in 2017; the song was written around the eight-year mark. The repetition makes the arithmetic feel like a verdict.

  2. Grieve my former self and the years I lost / Try to dig myself out, the fallacy of sunken cost

    The sunk-cost fallacy named outright. Staying because you have already given too much is the album's most uncomfortable idea, and the one it refuses to resolve.

About the song

Jack's skeleton of a song, originally a poem, originally about waiting for a train. Sam vetoed the literal version (“this isn’t Inception”) and the remaining train imagery became something more abstract: an empty platform, a train that may never come. Jono calls it the album's most metaphorical lyric and its loneliest song — no cleans, no dialogue, just one person.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. The tracks are empty / And around me only sky

    The title arrives as a kind of negative space. The song is a spiritual cousin to Carve Yourself Into My Lungs, except there is no second person left to address.

  2. This train has left the station / Is it ever coming back? / It’s never coming back

    The lyric's turn from question to answer is the whole song in three lines: hope, doubt, then the flat certainty that ends it.

About the song

The closer, and a deliberate exercise in simplicity. Jono has described it as “slow motion running in the rain, and the credits are about to roll” — a song with a happy sadness to it. It bookends Belrose: where the first track pulls someone else into the narrator's world, the last asks to be led instead.

  1. Take me by the hand / Show me who I am / I can’t see who I wanna be / One day I’ll just breathe

Album 2024

Maybe In Another Life

The debut album: a concept record about possibility, told in ten parts, and the release that took Bloom from Sydney rooms to the world.

Released
16 February 2024 Label
Pure Noise / Greyscale Records Producer
Christopher Vernon Tracks
10
Listen on Spotify Tracklist & lyrics↓
Tracklist 10 songs · 29 min 34 sec

Bloom's debut album is a concept record about possibility. Across ten tracks it follows a character negotiating longing, self-indulgence, fantasy, manipulation and despair, moving between soft-spoken atmosphere and some of the heaviest music the band had made.

It was written over roughly two years and recorded with producer Christopher Vernon, with the band treating everything from the visual identity to the colour palette as part of the same body of work. The album was released through Pure Noise Records in partnership with Greyscale Records.

The sequence is deliberately bookended: An Entry opens the door, and Through The Threshold, Beyond The Bend closes it with the same phrase the record is named for — “maybe in another life” — suggesting the cycle simply begins again.

Written from a demo dating back to March 2020, and the first step toward a heavier, more aggressive direction. Jono has described it as being about a manipulative partner whose allure is as destructive as it is irresistible, with the refrain “keep me in the dark” echoing the manipulation.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

    The refrain works as both a command and a confession. The narrator knows the manipulation is happening and still cannot step out of it.

  2. Tricked by your siren song / But now I hate the sound

    The title image resolves into disgust: the thing that drew them in is now unbearable. It is the album's cleanest expression of a spell breaking.

The first taste of solace on the record, and the point where anxiety starts to eat it. The song personifies self-doubt as a voice that arrives as a friend, then leeches off everything the narrator is not. Jarod's cleans trade lines with Jono's screams.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

Released in October 2024, between the debut album and the second, The Works Of You is a standalone single built around a museum metaphor: the heart as a gallery of everyone you have loved.

It arrived with a video and a headline Australian run that sold out, and became one of the band's most-streamed songs. Its central image — whether to scrape the paint off a memory or let it stay — belongs to the same emotional territory as The Light We Chase.

About the song

A song about keeping the art people leave behind in you. Jono has described it as being about the way people shape you permanently, and the impossible question of whether forgetting the pain would also erase the love.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. My heart is a museum of everyone I've loved

    The song's governing image, and one of Bloom's best. It turns memory into curation: some works stay on the wall, some get taken down.

  2. If I could just forget the pain and let the love remain / Then I would scratch and scrape away the paint

    The narrator wants to edit the memory rather than lose it. That impossibility is the song's whole tension.

About the song

The song's narrator begins by wanting to be anyone but themselves, and ends in a hole they cannot climb out of. The final line is a request rather than a resolution: “please give me a reason to stay”.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. Desperate to be like them / Or anyone but me

    The opening admission that later becomes “desperate to be like you” in Act II, the album's sequel to this song.

  2. Clouds turn into rain / I sink into the soil

    The title's image is passive and geological: the narrator is not buried so much as absorbed. It is a very different kind of despair from the rage on Siren Song.

About the song

A song about emotional numbness after a relationship ends, structured as a slow collapse: the same verse returns twice, but the second time the narrator has stopped asking whether the other person is okay and started asking to be left alone to hurt.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. I lost myself in someone else / I don't feel anything anymore

    The chorus states the song's real subject: not heartbreak, but the disappearance of feeling that follows it.

  2. Let me die / In the house we made

    The coda turns the shared home into a tomb. It is one of the earliest examples of Bloom using a physical place to carry an emotional idea, a technique they return to constantly on The Light We Chase.

About the song

The first Bloom song, and already obsessed with the idea of memory as something lodged in the body. The closing image of an empty passenger seat returns years later in Tongue Tied.

Song notes

Tap a highlighted lyric to jump between notes.

  1. Cure me of this disease / Erase you from my memory

    The title's plea frames love as illness and forgetting as the only cure — a metaphor Bloom never really abandoned.

  2. There's an empty space in the passenger seat

    The image that resurfaces in Tongue Tied (2025), where the seat is empty for a different reason. It is one of the longest threads in the band's catalogue.
